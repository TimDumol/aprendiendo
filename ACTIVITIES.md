The three I'd add first

1. Situational-response drills are probably the biggest missing piece.

Instead of:

> Translate: “I accompanied my friend to the police station yesterday.”



you'd get:

> Una amiga tuya tenía que ir a la comisaría y no quería ir sola. Cuéntame qué hiciste ayer.



And you might produce:

> Ayer acompañé a una amiga a la comisaría porque no quería ir sola...



That makes you retrieve acompañar a alguien, the preterite, gender agreement, etc. because you need them to express a thought—not because the English prompt told you exactly which structure to use.

Our Aprendiendo system already supports situational_response, and your current weak points are particularly well suited to it. I'd start making perhaps half of our targeted drills this type rather than translation.


---

2. Do lots of short unpredictable Q&A.

This trains something 4-3-2 doesn't: formulating a new answer immediately.

For example, I could fire six questions at you one at a time:

> ¿Cuál fue la última cosa que compraste y qué te pareció?
¿Qué haces normalmente cuando un restaurante está lleno?
Háblame de un sitio que buscabas pero que no encontraste.
¿Cuándo fue la última vez que acompañaste a alguien a algún sitio?



Give yourself perhaps 2–3 seconds before you must start speaking. You don't need an elegant answer. The skill you're training is:

idea → lexical retrieval → grammar → articulation

without constructing the sentence in English first.

As you improve, the prompts can become much more conversational and unpredictable.


---

3. Exploit your reading ability with “read → retell.”

This could be exceptionally efficient for you because you already consume Spanish well.

Read, say, one short El País article or 2–3 pages of a novel. Then shut it.

Don't summarize in writing. Explain it aloud:

> Pues, el artículo trata de…
Básicamente ha pasado que…
Lo que me ha parecido interesante es que…



Then, optionally, reopen the text and notice expressions the author used that you understood perfectly but couldn't retrieve yourself.

Those are fantastic Anki candidates because they represent a very specific gap:

passive knowledge → active knowledge.

Output itself can help expose these gaps: one proposed benefit of L2 production is precisely that it makes learners notice what they cannot yet express, rather than merely what they cannot understand. 

Don't abandon repetition—broaden it

4-3-2 isn't the only useful form of repeated speaking.

For example, you could tell a story today, receive corrections, and then tomorrow get a different prompt requiring similar language:

Day 1: Cuéntame lo que pasó en el concierto.
Day 2: Cuéntame una experiencia decepcionante en un restaurante.
Day 3: Háblame de un viaje que salió mejor de lo esperado.

That encourages transfer rather than memorizing one speech.

A 2025 meta-analysis found oral task repetition improved multiple dimensions of L2 production; exact repetition and narrative tasks were particularly useful in some measures. A 2026 review also notes a shift toward treating repetition as practice that can ultimately transfer to new speaking tasks. 

So we can use a progression like:

controlled sentence → situational response → related novel situation → spontaneous conversation

rather than repeatedly translating variants of the same sentence.
